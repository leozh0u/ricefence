Your first practice

You don't need to own anything. The club provides the equipment and lessons for new fencers, with coaches who have fenced and coached at the international level.

Wear athletic clothes, long pants if you have them, and bring water. We fence all three weapons, so ask to try whichever one you're curious about.

LPAP

You can also take fencing for credit. LPAP 172, Introduction to Fencing is a one-credit course that teaches movement, bladework and basic strategy in foil, and LPAP 173, Intermediate Fencing follows it. Club practice is separate from the course, so you can do either or both.

Three weapons

Foil

Point only. The target is the torso, covered by a conductive jacket called a lamé, and right of way decides the touch when both fencers hit.

Épée

Point only. The whole body is target, there is no right of way, and if both lights go on together, both fencers score.

Sabre

Cuts and thrusts. Everything above the waist counts, arms and head included, and right of way applies here too.

Right of way goes to the fencer who starts the attack. If that attack misses or gets parried, right of way passes to the other fencer. All three weapons are fenced on a 14-meter piste and scored electrically.

Questions

Do I need to have fenced before?

No. The club provides equipment and lessons for new fencers.

Do I need my own gear?

Not to start. The club lends masks, jackets and blades. If you stick with it, ask the officers what's worth buying first.

Is it dangerous?

It looks it, but the blades are blunt and everyone wears a mask, jacket and glove. Fencing has one of the lowest injury rates of any Olympic sport.

Do I need to be fit?

No. You'll get fitter, though. Your legs will let you know after your first footwork session.

Which weapon should I pick?

Whichever looks most fun. The club fences all three, and LPAP 172 teaches foil if you want a structured start. You can switch later.
